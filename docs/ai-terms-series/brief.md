## Editorial requirements

- Taiwan general readers; 1,800–3,000 characters of substantive running prose (paragraphs and lists, excluding tables, headings, references and link labels). Prefer 2,000–2,500 to leave margin. No generic repeated padding; every section must explain this particular term. Use at least three, usually five, H2 sections and concrete worked examples with input, steps, expected output and failure interpretation. No fabricated experiments or personal firsthand claims; examples are explicitly illustrative.
- Explain what it is, how it works, when useful, limitations, and neighboring concepts. Differentiate an agent's execution loop from engineering the system that schedules and verifies repeated agent runs; distinguish mechanisms from engineering disciplines. No invented universal definitions for emerging terms. Sources must identify whose meaning is used and where it varies.
- Search and read primary sources for EVERY article before writing. Normally at least two independent primary sources or an original paper plus official implementation documentation. Foundational definitions may use the canonical original specification plus an official application document. Record sources, access method/date, and claim mappings in research.json/notes.md. Avoid blanket citations that do not actually establish the claim. No copying an entire source's argument or paragraph structure; examples and teaching must be original. Do not silently use unavailable pages or date future content as checked.
- Reuse entries: read the existing pack, retain stable URL and useful assets if appropriate, then produce a complete revised pack in staging. Reverify factual claims and remove unnecessary volatile model names, prices or sweeping guarantees. Meet the same prose and visual requirements. Keep the article focused on the concept rather than turning it into a product feature roundup.
