## First-reader judgment

**Long video: 7/10.** The first sentence places an employee and a delivered tray in a Kentucky breakfast scene (`x180101`), and the same tray/route carries the listener through the pilot, expansion, pause, divergent company economics, and exit. The strongest dramatic turn is that McDonald's says the partnership met its expectations (`x180508`), while Krispy Kreme says demand per restaurant could not support daily delivery (`x180513`). That is a real answer to the opening question (`x180104`), not merely an ending statistic. Its arrival around 07:58 in an estimated 11-minute narration is late, but intervening openings and questions sustain interest.

The six chapters mostly advance in time and hand off a concrete object or question. Chapter 1 plants an unnamed October city, spring reconsideration, and June joint notice (`x180105`–`x180109`). Chapter 2 establishes the daily delivery obligation and the national ambition (`x180207`, `x180212`), though the three-flavor and box-size inventory (`x180205`–`x180206`) briefly stalls the cost story. Chapter 3 pays off Chicago and the year-end store count (`x180301`, `x180313`); its global-sales-point diversion asks who else is counted but answers only “other channels” (`x180316`–`x180318`). Chapter 4 turns a larger map into a deployment pause (`x180401`, `x180403`, `x180408`). Chapter 5 resolves the opposing business views (`x180508`, `x180513`). Chapter 6 gives the effective end date and later cost removal (`x180610`, `x180619`), but the quarter-end/global-count accounting excursion slows the final movement after the central answer.

The main excess is explanatory repetition: `x180605` already cautions that a global sales-point count is not a per-store profit ledger, and `x180620` says the same thing again without a named callback or new finding. `x180223` also announces what the script will inspect next. These are small local breaks rather than a collapse of the route story, but under the panel's one-break-is-NO rule they matter.

## Shorts, judged independently

**Short 1: 8/10.** Its opening question contrasts unexpectedly strong pilot demand with the eventual stop; 160 pilot restaurants, the national plan, roughly 2,400 reached restaurants, McDonald's satisfaction, and Krispy Kreme's delivery economics form a clean sequence. The final thought that pilot heat does not automatically become scale profit answers its own hook. The three-product delivery sentence adds useful daily cadence, although the national-plan beat could state even more plainly in narration that the 2026 target remained a plan.

**Short 2: 7/10.** Its 6/29 reporting cutoff and 7/2 effective termination give a satisfying, specific answer to why the still-active restaurants appear in the second-quarter count. The subsequent 14,851 global count, difference caveat, and separate footnote are careful, but introduce several quantities after that answer; the listener must work to distinguish all-channel totals from the approximately 2,400 partnership exits. The closing “check the statistical date” lands, with a more technical feel than Short 1.
