## Shorts: standalone story and 25–55 second window

Neither short has explicit line IDs or durations, so citations use its slug, beat and `shot`. The first short has 110 Han characters of narration across six scenes; the second has 128 across seven. At a natural Mandarin pace of roughly 3–4 Han characters per second, with brand names and short pauses added, the first plausibly takes about 35–48 seconds and the second about 40–54 seconds. Both plausibly fit 25–55 seconds, but the second is close to the upper edge if the read is intentionally slow; only timed audio can establish compliance.

**Short 1: standalone yes.** Its hook (`short-1`, `hook`, `x10-b04-s04`) asks whether eBay received sale cash; the stock distribution (`setup`, `x10-b04-s02`) and explicit no-cash answer (`proof`, `x10-b04-s05`) pay it off without requiring the long film. The `turn` (`x10-b04-s06`), `payoff` (`x10-b04-s13`) and final `loop` (`x10-b04-s07`) then repeat essentially the same continued-PayPal-service point. The last beat is a statement already given, not a fresh open loop. This is a pacing issue, not a failure to understand the short.

**Short 2: understandable on its own, but its promised answer is incomplete.** Its hook (`short-2`, `hook`, `x10-b01-s08`) asks *why* eBay took payment management in house after splitting PayPal. The 2018 start, 2019 adoption and later expansion (`setup` `x10-b05-s05`, `turn` `x10-b05-s08`, `proof` `x10-b06-s02`) answer *when/how it grew*. The only motive is eBay's qualified product claim (`proof` `x10-b06-s13`; `payoff` `x10-b06-s14`); the final 2021 all-transactions statement (`loop` `x10-b07-s03`) is another scale result. It never says the 2015 split left a PayPal operating agreement in place, so the leap from “split PayPal” to “took payments back” can imply a break in checkout that the long script expressly denies at `[x100406]`–`[x100407]`. I would not treat Short 2 as a complete standalone answer to its current why-hook, though its chronology and attribution are comprehensible.
