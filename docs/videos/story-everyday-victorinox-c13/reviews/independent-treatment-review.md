## Source readback and boundaries

- **S1, S9:** [Victorinox's history](https://www.victorinox.com/en/All-about-Victorinox/History/cms/history/) places the workshop in 1884, the officer's/sports knife patent in 1897, North American Swiss Army watch market entry with its former U.S. sales partner in **1989**, and licensed Travel Gear market entry with TRG in **1999**. It also lists the Wenger acquisition in 2005, acquisition of the travel-gear business from the former licensee in 2014, and Apparel discontinuation in 2017. S9 repeats this same history page plus S2; it is not another independent witness.
- **S2:** [SABI's 2001-07-30 8-K and exhibits](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/731947/000073194701500009/sabeightk.htm) distinguish SABI from Victorinox AG, identify AG as majority shareholder/manufacturer/provider, and announce an overseas watch-business combination before 9/11. The agreement contributes **existing** watch inventory and businesses; it does not say watches first launched that July.
- **S3:** [SABI's 2001 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/731947/000073194702000003/tenq.htm) identifies SABI's North American/Caribbean distribution territory, AG's approximately 69% ownership as of 2002-02-28, late-1999 Travel Gear launch, and VSA's 2001-11-01 operational start. Its 23% fall is **SABI's 2001 versus 2000 sales of Victorinox Original Swiss Army Knives, SwissCards, and SwissTools**; the filing also cites economic and corporate-market weakness and says it cannot quantify the event's contribution. Its 2001 watches and professional cutlery also declined, so do not narrate their later benefit as a proven 2001 SABI offset.
- **S4:** [SABI's 2002 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/731947/000073194702000016/tenq2.htm) gives Q2 year-on-year knife/tool −34%, watches +7%, professional cutlery +16%; H1 knife/tool −36% and watches +27%. H1 product sales also include a $3.7m one-customer promotion, and SABI *believes* airline restrictions hurt knife/tool sales. These are SABI product-sales comparisons, not Victorinox AG global revenue or profit.
- **S5 and contrary contemporaneous testimony:** [Swissinfo's 2002-01-29 English agency report](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/archive-banking-fintech/sales-blunted-at-victorinox/2511740) says knife sales “fell by almost a third in the final three months of 2001.” But [Swissinfo's 2002-01-25 Spanish original interview](https://www.swissinfo.ch/spa/archivo-finanzas/caen-ventas-de-navajas-suizas/2505136) quotes Victorinox export manager Paul Camenzin: “La cifra del 30% se limitó sólo al pasado mes de octubre.” The [Portuguese edition of that interview](https://www.swissinfo.ch/por/archive-financas/caem-as-vendas-de-canivetes-su%C3%AD%C3%A7os/2506316) likewise limits ~30% to October and reports a 7.5% full-year fall in the pocketknife segment, especially in the U.S.; these two language editions are **one interview**, not two corroborating witnesses. Camenzin emphasizes post-9/11 consumer behavior, tourism and recession over new security measures as the sales cause. The January 29 report still supports that many airports stopped knife sales, hand baggage was affected, and Victorinox publicly discussed product and check-in-luggage responses. It does **not** resolve the October-versus-Q4 denominator conflict.
- **S6:** In [Carl Elsener's later first-person interview](https://www.swissmadebusiness.com/brands/victorinox), he recalls duty-free/on-board collapse, a 30% pocketknife-turnover drop and temporarily lending up to 80 staff for 4–8 months to avoid layoffs. This is recollection, without a dated HR roster or a specified quarter.
- **S7:** In [Blick's 2024 interview with Elsener](https://www.blick.ch/wirtschaft/victorinox-boss-carl-elsener-im-interview/e7cty4f), he says 9/11 showed the risk of relying on one line and that existing watches, professional knives and kitchen knives helped. This is his management judgment, not an independently measured recovery by category.
- **S8:** I downloaded and extracted the one-page [TSA historical comparison PDF](https://www.tsa.gov/sites/default/files/9766_info_aviation_security_then_now_928.pdf): it places small knives/box cutters among pre-9/11 allowed items. I separately read the [live TSA knife list](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/all?combine=knife&field_item_category_value=All&page=0) on 2026-10-22 UTC: Swiss Army Knife is No for carry-on and Yes for checked baggage. These U.S. current/historical summaries cannot establish every airport's 2001 rule or give contemporary cross-border travel advice.

## Treatment and production-rule audit

The six chapters form a chronological, usable reversal: 1884/1897 origin → **1989 watches and 1999 licensed Travel Gear** → July 2001 watch agreement → 2001–02 shock and responses → later portfolio choices. It keeps AG and SABI visibly separate, labels the CEO's hindsight, has a concrete cold open, chapter-end questions and planned 30–45 second hooks, places the five-second intro after the cold open, and reserves the 21-second v4 outro/link area. The proposed illustrations and thumbnail use generic objects, later-added text, no logo/real face/attack depiction, and specify MiniMax image-01 with exact {"preset":"cute-mystery"}. The plan states ≥70% illustrations and ≤3 consecutive cards, but no shot list, measured narration, actual loops or paid-image quote exists yet.

The claimed **9:05 body is only an outline estimate**. The origin and especially the 7:15–9:10 “now” chapter have few independent event details beyond milestone dates. Eight source-backed narration minutes are plausible if the SEC filing's business structure and contemporaneous responses are explained, but are not demonstrated by this Treatment. If later script passages need unsupported filler, shorten rather than asserting an eight-minute pass.
