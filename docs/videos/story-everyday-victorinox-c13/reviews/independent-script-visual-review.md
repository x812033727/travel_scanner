## Direct source readback

I reopened the publisher's pages and original filings, rather than accepting the author's matrix or notes as evidence:

- **S1/S9:** [Victorinox's timeline](https://www.victorinox.com/en-CA/All-about-Victorinox/History/cms/history/) directly places the workshop in 1884, the officers' and sports knife patent in 1897, U.S. Army Post Exchange orders in 1945, North American watches in 1989, licensed travel gear in 1999, Wenger acquisition in 2005, acquisition of the former licensee's travel-gear business in 2014, and apparel discontinuation in 2017. Its 2001 clothing milestone gives no month or 9/11 cause. S9 duplicates S1 and S2.
- **S2–S4:** The [2001-07-30 SABI 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/731947/000073194701500009/sabeightk.htm) announces combining *already existing* watch operations outside North America before 9/11. The [SABI 2001 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/731947/000073194702000003/tenq.htm) says Victorinox AG is SABI's Swiss supplier and controlling shareholder, the watch company began operating on 2001-11-01, and **SABI's** Original Swiss Army Knives/SwissCards/SwissTools sales fell 23% in 2001 year on year; it also describes recession, corporate-channel and other factors without quantifying each cause. The [SABI 2002 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/731947/000073194702000016/tenq2.htm) gives **SABI's Q2 product-category year-on-year** knife/tool −34%, watches +7%, professional cutlery +16%; its H1 sales also reflect a one-customer promotion. These are not Victorinox AG global revenue or profit figures.
- **S5/S10:** [Swissinfo's 2002-01-29 agency report](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/archive-banking-fintech/sales-blunted-at-victorinox/2511740) says many airport knife sales stopped and reports a near-third knife-sales fall in **2001 Q4**. The [2002-01-25 direct Camenzin interview in Spanish](https://www.swissinfo.ch/spa/archive-finanzas/caen-ventas-de-navajas-suizas/2505136), cross-read with its [Portuguese edition](https://www.swissinfo.ch/por/archive-financas/caem-as-vendas-de-canivetes-su%C3%AD%C3%A7os/2506316), says roughly 30% applied **only to October**. The two editions are one interview, not independent corroboration. Camenzin's ranking of changed consumption/tourism and recession ahead of new security measures is his attribution, not a measured causal split. S5 separately distinguishes a clock knife being rushed, a rounded children's knife being developed, bladeless tools already offered, and checked-bag brochures already issued; it proves no sales recovery.
- **S6/S7:** [Elsener's 2012 first-person interview](https://www.swissmadebusiness.com/brands/victorinox) recalls lending up to 80 workers for 4–8 months to avoid layoffs. His [2024 Blick interview](https://www.blick.ch/wirtschaft/victorinox-boss-carl-elsener-im-interview/e7cty4f) calls pre-existing watches, professional knives and kitchen knives helpful. Both are retrospective management accounts, not 2001–02 audited Victorinox AG results. I additionally reopened his [2025 first-person interview](https://slman.com/life/interviews-careers/carl-elsener), which says 60 people were lent for several months. This independently supports the existence of differing recollected counts, but **the authored S6 citation alone does not support that comparison**.
- **S8:** Direct fetch of the [TSA historical PDF](https://www.tsa.gov/sites/default/files/9766_info_aviation_security_then_now_928.pdf) and [current list](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/all?combine=knife&field_item_category_value=All&page=0) returned 403 in this review; indexed TSA text describes pre-9/11 small knives versus the current prohibited list. Neither is used in the 75 narration lines. I did not substitute a present-day U.S. rule for a particular 2001 airport.
