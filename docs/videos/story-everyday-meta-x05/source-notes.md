### Exact source passages and denominators

- **M21, filing introduction:** “In October 2021, we changed our corporate name from Facebook, Inc. to Meta Platforms, Inc.” This establishes the month and corporate name only; it does not prove that a particular product or division started that month.
- **M21, Executive Overview of Full Year 2021 Results:** “Beginning in the fourth quarter of 2021, we report our financial results based on two reportable segments: Family of Apps (FoA) and Reality Labs (RL). FoA includes Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, and other services. RL includes our augmented and virtual reality related consumer hardware, software, and content.” This establishes the *reporting change*, not the birth of RL or all of its investments.
- **M21, business description:** “For FoA, we generate substantially all of our revenue from selling advertising placements to marketers.” Do not equate FoA operating income with ad revenue; costs still exist.
- **M22, Segment profitability / Family of Apps:** “FoA income from operations in 2022 decreased $14.29 billion, or 25%, compared to 2021.” The filing attributes the drop to higher FoA costs and expenses, primarily payroll and related costs from more employees, restructuring charges, and higher data-center / technical-infrastructure costs. The FY2022 and FY2021 operating-income table values are US$42,661 million and US$56,946 million, so the precise difference is US$14,285 million; the prose rounds to US$14.29 billion.
- **M22, Consolidated and Segment Results:** FoA **revenue** was US$114,450 million in FY2022 versus US$115,655 million in FY2021 (−US$1,205 million, −1%); FoA **costs and expenses** were US$71,789 million versus US$58,709 million (+US$13,080 million, +22%). The same-table operating-income decline is US$14,285 million: −1,205 − 13,080 = −14,285. The section lists total FY2022 restructuring charges of US$4,611 million, of which the prose allocates US$4.10 billion to FoA and US$515 million to RL. Rounded prose values must not be summed as if exact, and the company total must not be assigned solely to FoA.
- **M22, Headcount / Restructuring:** “Headcount was 86,482 as of December 31, 2022, an increase of 20% year-over-year.” The filing immediately adds that this **companywide year-end count** includes “a substantial majority of the approximately 11,000 employees impacted by the layoff we announced in November 2022.” November dates the **announcement**, not completion of every employee departure. The restructuring description names facilities consolidation and cancellation of multiple data-center projects. Do not imply +20% is FoA-specific headcount or that all affected people had already left in November.
- **M22, Segment profitability / Reality Labs:** “RL loss from operations in 2022 increased $3.52 billion, or 35%, compared to 2021.” The filing attributes the wider loss mainly to increased payroll and related expenses and R&D expenses, partly offset by reduced RL inventory cost, including lower losses on purchase commitments. The table's loss magnitudes are US$13,717 million and US$10,193 million, giving a precise US$3,524 million increase; the prose rounds to US$3.52 billion.
- **M22, Revenue / Family of Apps and Advertising:** FoA revenue fell US$1.21 billion / 1% versus FY2021, mostly driven by ads; ad revenue fell US$1.29 billion / 1%, with a 16% lower average ad price partly offset by more ads delivered. These are revenue / price / volume changes, not the operating-income changes above.
- **M23, business description:** “Currently, we generate substantially all of our revenue from selling advertising placements on our family of apps to marketers, which is reflected in FoA.” The qualifier “substantially all” applies to **revenue**, not operating income or cash flow.
- **M23, Revenue / Advertising:** “Advertising revenue in 2023 increased $18.31 billion, or 16%, compared to 2022 due to an increase in the number of ads delivered, partially offset by a decrease in the average price per ad.” Ads delivered increased **28%** and average price per ad fell **9%**, both versus FY2022. Those are advertising revenue and price/volume metrics, distinct from the +47% FY2023 FoA operating-income change.
- **M22, Note 3 / Restructuring sequence:** From Q3 2022 the company **decided** to sublease, terminate early, or abandon several leased office buildings. It announced layoffs affecting approximately 11,000 employees in November. In December it decided to pivot several data-center building projects to a next-generation design and cancel multiple existing projects, citing efficiency and AI. These are decision and announcement dates, not evidence that every lease ended, employee departed, or redesigned site opened in that year. Exact original passages and independent notes are in [event-research-v7.md](reviews/event-research-v7.md).
- **M23, Advertising mechanism:** The filing attributes more ads delivered to more ads and greater frequency across products plus more users. It attributes the lower average price partly to increased impressions in lower-monetizing geographies and products such as Reels. Reels is an example of mix, not a sole cause or a disclosed product price.
- **M23, 2023 Restructuring / Marketing and sales:** Three planned layoff rounds covering approximately 10,000 people across FoA and RL were announced in March and completed by December 31; companywide year-end headcount was **67,317**, down **22%** year over year. Marketing and sales expense fell **US$2.96 billion / 19%** versus FY2022, mainly from lower promotion and payroll-related expense; the latter was linked to lower headcount in marketing and sales functions. The filing does **not** attribute all of the expense decline or all headcount change to the March layoffs.
- **M25, Advertising mechanism:** FY2025 versus FY2024 ad impressions delivered increased **12%**, average price per ad increased **9%**, and advertising revenue increased **US$35.54 billion / 22%**. These are FY2024 comparisons; they do not prove a monotone FY2023–FY2025 path. Meta **believes** targeting and measurement improvements increased ad demand, partly offset by lower-monetizing inventory; retain the company attribution.
- **M25, Reality Labs Products launch:** In FY2025 Meta introduced **Meta Ray-Ban Display**, an AI-glasses model with a display integrated into the lens and a wrist-worn **Meta Neural Band** that uses electromyography to control the glasses. The filing does not report that model's sales or operating profit, or establish that it caused the division's aggregate AI-glasses sales growth. A separate FY2026 70%/30% RL expense allocation in the filing is forward-looking and is excluded from the script.
- **M25, business description:** “RL revenue is generated from the delivery of consumer hardware products, such as Meta Quest and AI glasses, and related software and content.” FY2025 RL cannot be described as VR headsets or “metaverse” alone.
- **M25, Reality Labs Products:** “Many of our Reality Labs investments are directed toward long-term, cutting edge research and development for products that are not on the market today and may only be fully realized in the next decade.” This is company expectation, not independently verified future success.
- **M25, Revenue and Investments:** “In 2025, 82% of our total costs and expenses were recognized in FoA and 18% were recognized in RL.” This is a split of **FY2025 company costs and expenses**, not revenue, profit, cash flow, or ROI. The same paragraph says “our ability to support our efforts to build the next computing platform is dependent on generating sufficient profits from other areas of our business.” Attribute that dependence to Meta's own statement; it does not document earmarked FoA cash transfers or guarantee RL investment returns.

The figures below are from each filing's **“Segment profitability / income (loss) from operations by segment”** table, in **US$ millions for the fiscal year ended December 31**. They are *operating income/loss*, not revenue, net income, cash spent, free cash flow, or capital expenditure. Parentheses in the SEC table mark a loss.

Across the two endpoint years FY2021 and FY2023, FoA operating income rose by **US$5,925 million** while the magnitude of the RL operating loss rose by **US$5,927 million**. The consolidated operating result therefore moved only **−US$2 million**, from 46,753 to 46,751. The intervening FY2022 company result was **28,944**, a **US$17,809 million decline** from FY2021 followed by a **US$17,807 million rebound** into FY2023; the near-equal endpoints did not mean a flat intervening year. Between the FY2023 and FY2025 endpoints, FoA rose by **US$39,598 million** and the magnitude of the RL loss rose by **US$3,073 million**; total operating income rose by **US$36,525 million**. Endpoint differences are not an uninterrupted trend, a cash-transfer record, or evidence that one segment caused the other's result.

Supporting exact table heading in M25: “The following table sets forth income (loss) from operations by segment: Year Ended December 31, 2025 2024 2023 ... (in millions, except percentages)”. The M25 explanation after the table says the 2025 RL operating loss grew by US$1.46 billion versus 2024, driven by higher RL costs and expenses. It identifies employee compensation, estimated losses on non-cancelable RL inventory commitments, and technology development costs among the drivers; do not replace those with a single “headset flop” cause. M23 gives 2023 RL operating loss US$16.120 billion; the decline in RL revenue was one cited driver that year, along with personnel costs.

M25 separately says **RL revenue in FY2025 rose US$61 million, or 3%, versus FY2024**, driven by increased AI-glasses sales, partially offset by lower Meta Quest sales. The revenue increase and the larger operating loss have different denominators and cannot be combined into a claim that AI glasses caused the loss. An independent reviewer verified this wording in the saved M25 HTML on 2026-10-20.

M23 says FY2023 **FoA operating income rose US$20.21 billion, or 47%, versus FY2022**, mainly driven by higher advertising revenue and lower marketing and sales expenses. It separately says FY2023 RL operating loss rose US$2.40 billion, or 18%, versus FY2022, mainly due to increased personnel-related expenses and decreased RL revenue. M23 reports FY2023 **RL revenue down US$263 million, or 12%, versus FY2022**, mostly driven by a *net decrease in the volume of Meta Quest sales*. The filing does not give a unit count in that sentence. These one-year explanations do not explain the entire FY2021→FY2023 endpoint difference.

## Six-chapter source seed

This compact source outline preceded the event-led production treatment. The current six-chapter sequence, including FY2022 restructuring decisions, FY2023 ad mix and staffing, and FY2025 ad demand and product sales divergence, is in [treatment.md](treatment.md); the independent evidence for those additions is [event-research-v7.md](reviews/event-research-v7.md).

1. **The same-page contradiction.** FY2025 RL operating loss is nearly US$19.2 billion while the company operating result is positive. Conceal the precise company total. Preview distinct unresolved FY2022 ad price/delivery and cost lines and FY2025 product/cost lines without giving their outcomes.
2. **The 2021 reporting split.** Return to the October 2021 rename and Q4 start of two reportable segments, without implying RL was created then. Separate FoA advertising revenue from operating income; reveal FY2021 FoA +56,946, RL −10,193, company +46,753 (US$ millions).
3. **The 2022 dip.** M22 says FY2022 ad average price fell 16% with ads delivered rising, and FoA ad revenue was down US$1.29 billion/1%; FoA operating income fell to +42,661 (US$ millions), attributed mainly to higher personnel, restructuring, data-center and infrastructure costs. RL loss deepened to −13,717, mainly from personnel and R&D costs partly offset by lower inventory cost. Company operating income fell to +28,944.
4. **The 2023 rebound and near-equal endpoint.** FoA operating income rose to +62,871, partly due to higher ad revenue and lower marketing/sales expenses; RL loss deepened to −16,120, partly due to personnel costs and lower RL revenue. RL revenue fell US$263 million/12%, mostly from fewer Quest sales by volume. Company operating income returned to +46,751, only US$2 million below FY2021.
5. **The path inside the endpoints.** The FY2021→FY2023 FoA gain of +5,925 and RL loss expansion of +5,927 (US$ millions) nearly offset, but the FY2022 dip was real: company operating income fell US$17,809 million then rose US$17,807 million. FY2023→FY2025 endpoints have FoA +39,598 and RL loss magnitude +3,073, no longer near equal. These comparisons are arithmetic, not a continuous trend or cash-transfer record.
6. **Return to FY2025.** M25 says RL revenue grew US$61 million/3% from FY2024 as AI-glasses sales increased and Quest sales decreased, while RL operating loss rose US$1.46 billion due to higher costs, including personnel, estimated noncancelable inventory commitment losses and technology development. Attribute the longer-term research expectation to the company. Only at the end reveal the same-year operating-income equation: FoA +102,469 − RL 19,193 = company +83,276 (US$ millions); then one viewer question and one subscription reason.
