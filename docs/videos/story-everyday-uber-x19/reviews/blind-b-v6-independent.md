## Six-chapter flow and payoffs

1. **April driver:** A driver replaces a passenger wait with a food-bag wait. The opening asks whether June's apparent delivery lead meant profit, what July's second network was, whether the networks joined, and whether rides would lead again. It states the April driver crossover as the scene, not the financial outcome.
2. **Earlier baseline:** The story steps back to the existing delivery network and 2019–2020 Q1 bookings, then follows an available driver, restaurant supply, and market choices toward the unanswered question of a platform-wide crossover. This backward step is legible because the first line explicitly reconnects to the driver.
3. **June crossover:** Delivery bookings pass rides; the script distinguishes platform bookings, company revenue, adjusted segment EBITDA, and company net loss. The opened profit question pays off as a delivery segment loss. The chapter ends by asking whether Postmates caused the crossover.
4. **July signature and autumn:** The July 6 agreement comes after Q2, and Q3 delivery still leads before closing. This answers the causation question. The passage then tracks the still-developing delivery route and asks whether the second network has joined by year-end.
5. **December close and later migration:** The acquisition closes, the consumer Apps initially remain separate, and roughly five million consumers are said to migrate by 2021 Q2. Delivery still leads in bookings while its adjusted segment EBITDA remains negative. That distinction sets up the year-end profit question.
6. **Later reversal:** Delivery's adjusted segment EBITDA turns positive in 2021 Q4. Rides regain the booking lead in 2022 Q4 while delivery itself grows, and both adjusted segment figures are positive. The roadside driver and the two uses of one phone return, closing the opening's last question without implying that segment figures equal company net profit.

The strongest callbacks are explicit: the opening bag at `x190317`, the unopened network at `x190401` and `x190501`, and the opening car at `x190611`. The lines at `x190403` and `x190412` usefully clarify that a later acquisition cannot cause the already-ended June quarter. The actual redundancy is narrower: `x190421` repeats `x190412`'s still-unclosed status in the same autumn passage. Cutting 「收購仍只是紙上的約定」 from `x190421` would leave its new App fact and the year-end hook intact.
