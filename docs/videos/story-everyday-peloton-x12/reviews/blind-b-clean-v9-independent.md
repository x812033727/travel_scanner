## Narrative stress points beyond the binary panel

The first line gives an exact date and a physical factory plan. The first real conflict is `[x120104]`; the opening's later hooks are identifiable questions, although the restructuring-file and accounting-file language is more abstract than a vivid acted scene. The script remains legible as one Ohio chronology. Its one short step backward to the April 2021 Precor acquisition explains what manufacturing capacity was already in place before the May proposal; it does not repeat a prior event sequence.

The clearest editorial repetition is the February sale plan at `[x120401]` and `[x120412]`. Later references must be distinguished from it: `[x120511]` adds a **September 2022** marketing status, and `[x120612]` adds the **FY2023** still-unsold status, so those are real updates. The backlog discussion from `[x120201]` through `[x120213]` spends thirteen sentences on parts, freight, delays, cancellation, and cost before the Precor turn. Each has a causal use, but the stretch is dense and could feel like a risk catalogue to a listener.

The FY2021 hardware revenue surge appears at `[x120204]`, and accelerated shipping and higher component costs at `[x120208–x120213]`. No FY2021 equipment gross margin or gross-loss figure is spoken. The FY2022 and FY2023 equipment gross losses at `[x120413]` and `[x120607]` cannot establish the FY2021 margin effect. That is a narrower quantitative payoff than the setup might lead a listener to expect, even though the operational question at `[x120209]` is answered by the existing manufacturing network and its limits at `[x120210–x120213]`. The Chapter 5 to 6 question, by contrast, is clearly paid off: `[x120519]` asks whether subscription growth restores Ohio's work, and `[x120601–x120606]` explains why it does not.
