## Coverage and causal limits

- **Plan and physical site** — `x120101–109`, `x120301–319`, the main title/thumbnail and Short 1 opening: A21 supports Ohio site selection, first planned dedicated US facility, 2023 intended production, the multiyear ~$400m commitment, conditional approvals and proposed size. K21 supports existing Asian contractors and Precor facilities. K22 supports the later marketing decision and $86.6m development spend. The thumbnail's question “廠沒開？” is consistent with a planned manufacturing use later abandoned, so long as visuals remain conceptual rather than purported Ohio documentary images.
- **Business model and earlier pressure** — `x120201–221`: K21 supports separate product/subscription streams; FY2021 backlog, expedites, higher component/freight costs; contractor procurement and Precor's April 2021 acquisition. The linkage between demand growth and urgent logistics is in K21 MD&A. The Ohio facility is a proposed capacity response, not a proven solution to single-source parts or freight. The “same order” phrase above is the exception.
- **FY2020–FY2022 figures and causes** — `x120204–207`, `x120401–426` and Short 2: K22's **same three-year table** gives product revenue $1,462.2m/$3,149.6m/$2,187.5m, subscription revenue $363.7m/$872.2m/$1,394.7m, and total revenue $1,825.9m/$4,021.8m/$3,582.1m. FY2022 product gross profit is **$(246.3)m**, subscription gross profit **$944.7m**, and product gross margin moves **29.0% to (11.3)%**. Spoken Chinese roundings and all corresponding cards check. K22 attributes product revenue decline chiefly to fewer Bike/Tread+ deliveries and recall charges, partly offset by more Tread deliveries and $186.6m Precor commercial revenue; the draft preserves that direction without assigning the whole change to Bike demand. K22 links product costs to **$222.1m increase in inventory reserves** (especially excess inventory and obsolete restructuring raw materials), logistics/port/storage, pricing and recalls. Subscription cost rose $119.5m, including $83.6m more music royalties/platform streaming. Category gross profit is not company net profit or bike units.
- **Manufacturing turn and Ohio accounting** — `x120501–523` and Short 1: A22 supports the July 12 owned-manufacturing exit announcement, existing Rexon relationship, future Bike/Tread primary role and **Taiwan Tonic** suspension for the remainder of 2022. K22 says July's shift was announced for 100% third-party production and contractors would follow Peloton designs/specifications. February restructuring predates July; FY2022 ended June 30, 12 days before the July release. K22's $86.6m is invested **capital expenditure** as of June 30, not the planned $400m and not proceeds. Its $40.3m impairment covers Output Park **and related manufacturing assets**; subtraction cannot produce a sale value. K22 explicitly warns recovery is uncertain. Neither A22 nor K22 says Ohio was closed or sold.
- **FY2023 endpoint** — `x120601–617`: K23 verifies product revenue $1,130.2m, subscription revenue $1,670.1m, product category gross loss $198.6m, subscription category gross profit $1,122.1m and overall net loss $1,261.7m. The draft correctly does not equate growing subscriptions with overall profitability. K23 still says Ohio was being marketed for sale. Its “lower than forecasted demand ... excess manufacturing capacity” risk language and K22's excess inventory make the draft's fixed-capacity tension a defensible **editorial inference**; the filings do not quantify which factor caused the Ohio sale decision. `x120612` properly rejects a single-cause claim. Nothing in these sources proves a transaction after FY2023.
