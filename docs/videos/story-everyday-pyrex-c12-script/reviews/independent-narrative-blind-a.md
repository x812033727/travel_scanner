## What works

The opening asks how that path led to products bearing the same name but different materials (H01–H06). The handoffs from the industrial problem to Bessie's trial, a purpose-built product, and buyer demonstrations are understandable (O05–O12, I01–I04, E03–E05, E16–T03).

## Revision needed

1. **Restore a question at the turn into the final chapter.** T15 asks whether today's same-name dish is still the 1915 glass, but T16 immediately gives the organizing answer — era, region, and use — before N01 begins. Let the next chapter reveal that framework through a concrete contrast, or end T16 on a specific unresolved consequence. The current boundary spends its cliffhanger one line early (T15–T16, N01–N03).
2. **Tighten repeated setup and trust explanation.** The hot-lamp/cold-weather problem is stated three ways in O02–O04. The distance from factory to home is restated in O08–O10. E09–E13 circle the buyer's doubt before E16 supplies the next action. Keep the sharpest version of each obstacle and move sooner to the person or decision that changes it. The middle will then sound like events causing the next event rather than repeated framing (O02–O04, O08–O12, E09–E16).
3. **Give the modern comparison a stronger story rhythm.** N01–N11 spend roughly 398 narration characters on successive US, French, and laboratory examples. Several lines resolve the question immediately or state a limitation, so the listener has little pending question for about a minute and a half at the repository's 250 characters/minute planning rate. Make each example change the listener's working answer and carry one unanswered question forward to N10–N11. Preserve any source-required qualifications when revising (N01–N11).
4. **Make the spoken ending comply with the series rule.** N14 answers with a useful action and N15 poses a related viewer question. N16 then promises what will happen “next time,” although the brand-story rule says the narration must not announce a next video. Keep the subscription invitation but close on this story (N14–N16).
