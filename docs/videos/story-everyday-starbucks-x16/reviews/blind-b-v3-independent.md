The 2008 action is concrete in its key distinctions. `x160301–x160327` separates the July announcement from an immediate closure, gives store-level profitability, acceptable future return, and market/site conditions, and admits the absence of public store-by-store reasons. `x160401–x160410` distinguishes 205 actual FY2008 closures from the 600 target and the +445 overall net change. The denominator guard at `x160305–x160307` makes the “seven in ten” statistic precise: it describes selected stores, not all U.S. stores or all recently opened stores. These are editorial strengths worth preserving.

The main structural cost is answer timing. The rule is already clear by `x160314`, while `x160618–x160621` repeats it. The actual July cohort total arrives at `x160505`, then `x160641` restates it. A viewer can therefore leave before the closing line without losing the promised selection-rule/588 answer. The separate 2009 group is handled carefully at `x160501–x160509`; its 183 closures are not folded into 588. The 2010 +7% same-store result is also carefully limited: `x160602–x160616` define the cohort, exclude the 53rd week from that percentage, and reject sole causal credit for closures. The proof takes a long detour through several other metrics.

The body from `x160201` through `x160630` contains 1,960 Han characters across 142 narration lines. At 4.3–4.5 Han characters per second plus the reference's default roughly 0.3-second inter-line pause, it lands around **8:00–8:20**; actual voiced duration still needs measurement. It is close enough to the eight-minute minimum that pacing should be checked on audio, not inferred from the `[8,12]` target field. The closing `x160641–x160643` follows the required three-line structure and asks a viewer-answerable profit-versus-future-return question, although its answer sentence is recap rather than a new release.

Both Shorts read as standalone, different 25–55-second stories at normal spoken pace. Short 1 uses the roughly 600 selected U.S. stores and seven-in-ten newly opened cohort to ask why a new store could be chosen; it defines the denominator and ends with the economic selection criteria. Its 154 Han characters estimate to roughly 36–45 seconds with pauses. Short 2 asks whether closures alone explain the FY2009 −6% to FY2010 +7% same-store swing, then defines eligible stores, handles the 53rd week, cites transactions/ticket, and declines single-cause attribution. Its 140 Han characters estimate to roughly 33–42 seconds. They share the episode but make distinct claims, and neither requires the long video to make sense. Short 1 spends two beats negating denominator misreadings, which slightly slows its payoff.

## Subsequent visual and portrait-plan check

After recording the score above, I read `visual-plan.md` and `shorts-portrait-plan.md`, and checked the relevant shot/card fields in the frozen JSON. The plans explicitly describe the pictures as anonymous concepts, not reconstructions of a particular 2008 store, list, or worker. The 87 shot prompts use generic, unbranded places, blank ledgers, shop tokens, and fictional people; I found no named closed store or recognizable Schultz depiction. For example, the opening notice at `x16-b01-s01`, the selected-token scene at `x16-b03-s04`, and the closed-key/mature-token scene at `x16-b06-s10` are symbolic actions. This avoids implying that the source identifies a particular closed address.

All 16 Short beats reuse **16 distinct `shot` scenes**, and every selected prompt includes the explicit instruction to keep face, hands, and focal prop in the central 608-pixel corridor for a later 9:16 crop. The portrait table supplies a per-beat 608×1080 window, native headline, and safe zones for headline and subtitles. Those are sound production cues; actual focal placement after generation and the 360-pixel mobile preview remain unverified. Some planned x positions shift the 608-pixel crop by up to about 46 pixels from the exact central corridor, so the promised per-shot crop check matters.

The ten long-form stat cards put the exact numbers in post-produced text, while the Shorts reuse illustrations and place their own text natively in 9:16. This avoids shrinking a horizontal stats card into a Short. **Small-card legibility remains a production check**, not a demonstrated pass: several 1080p long-form cards combine a title, a value, a 18–23-character qualifier, and a source footer (`x16-b01-s02`, `x16-b03-s18`, `x16-b05-s03`). At phone width, the qualifier and `S2`/`S3/S4` source notation may be hard to read or opaque. The plans request a 360-pixel preview, but no rendered card or mobile frame is available here, so I cannot certify its type size or contrast.
