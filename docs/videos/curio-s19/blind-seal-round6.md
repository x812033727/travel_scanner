## 13 independent comprehension answers from the script

1. **What begins the story?** A Paris newspaper published artists' protest against the not-yet-standing tower in February 1887; Eiffel said its foundations had already been laid (L9–21).
2. **Whom did the artists ask to act?** They addressed Alphand and asked him to stop the work on Paris's behalf (L43–47).
3. **What did the artists claim to defend?** Paris's art and history, represented through streets, riverbanks, and old stone buildings (L51–60).
4. **What did their threatening tower image mean?** They imagined a dark industrial chimney dwarfing Notre-Dame, the Louvre, and the Arc de Triomphe; this was a projected scene, not a measured shadow (L76–100).
5. **What was the petition's “twenty years”?** A feared duration under that imagined tower shadow, not an asserted demolition date (L25, L100–104).
6. **What first surprised Eiffel in the interview?** Architect Garnier's signature; Eiffel said Garnier had served on the tower committee and approved its proceedings (L126–134).
7. **Why did Eiffel say the objection arrived late?** He said opponents could have joined discussion a year earlier, whereas the contract had now been signed and iron ordered (L138–147).
8. **What financial figures did Eiffel give?** An estimated five to six million francs to build and a state subsidy of 1.5 million francs (L163, L169–170).
9. **What did the twenty-year right cover?** Eiffel described operating the tower during the exposition, then receiving a twenty-year operating right from Paris; afterward the city would control it. The interview supplies no clause forcing demolition (L178–207).
10. **How did Eiffel answer criticism of the tower's looks?** He said it was too early to judge a structure known only from geometric drawings, defended the wind-calculated curves as potentially beautiful, and disputed that it would spoil views at Notre-Dame (L229–279).
11. **What uses did Eiffel propose, and had they happened yet?** Meteorological, astronomical, physical, and wartime communication uses were possibilities he argued for before completion, not achieved results in that interview (L301–309, L333–341).
12. **What later use did the 1913 notice record?** It reported repeated radio exchanges between the tower and a Washington station, over an estimated 6,000 km, with roughly 80 and 90 horsepower respectively; it also proposed a later longitude measurement (L349–408).
13. **What conclusion can the presented newspapers support about demolition?** They show a limited operating right and later practical communication, but no demolition order, municipal decision, or causal proof that radio saved the tower (L198–207, L412–428).
