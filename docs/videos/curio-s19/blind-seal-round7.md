## Thirteen cold-viewer comprehension answers

1. **What happened in February 1887?** A Paris newspaper printed artists' protest against a tower that had not yet risen (lines 9–21).
2. **What did the artists fear about 20 years?** They imagined Paris under the tower's oppressive shadow for that long; the number is their forecast in the letter (lines 25, 100–104).
3. **Who was asked to act?** The letter addressed Alphand and asked him to protect Paris by stopping the work (lines 43–47).
4. **Which landmarks did they fear would look diminished?** Notre-Dame, the Louvre, and the Arc de Triomphe (lines 76–88).
5. **Was that shadow an observed measurement?** No; it was the petition's imagined future, as line 100 explicitly says.
6. **What did Eiffel say made immediate cancellation late?** He said the contract was signed and iron ordered; his answer is reported at lines 139–147.
7. **What financial terms did Eiffel report?** Estimated construction cost of five to six million francs and a 1.5-million-franc state subsidy (lines 169–170).
8. **What did his 20-year right cover?** Eiffel's reported operating concession from the City of Paris after the exhibition, following operation during the exposition and handover of the tower to the city (lines 178–199).
9. **Did that statement establish automatic demolition?** No. He reportedly said that after expiry the city would own and dispose of the tower; the narration has no contract text or explicit demolition duty (lines 198–207).
10. **Why did Eiffel say permanent judgment was premature?** The tower was unbuilt and viewers only had geometrical drawings, not the finished object (lines 229–242).
11. **Which possible uses did Eiffel name?** Meteorology, astronomy and physics research, and keeping Paris connected to other parts of France in wartime; they were predictions, not achieved results (lines 301–309, 333–341).
12. **What did the 1913 newspaper report?** Repeated wireless exchanges between the tower station and a Washington station, over an estimated 6,000 km; it also contemplated measuring longitude (lines 353–404).
13. **What can the two newspaper accounts prove about the tower's survival?** They document a reported 20-year operating term and a later practical wireless use, but contain neither a demolition order nor a city decision or causal record proving radio saved the tower (lines 198–207, 412–428).
