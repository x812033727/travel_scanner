## Fact review

- **Landing, doors and search:** Moore's letter dated 28 December 1900 states that the flag was absent, the ship sounded its horn, he first landed while McCormack's party waited in the boat, the entrance gate and outside door were shut, the kitchen door was open, the fire had not burned for days, and he ran back to report the empty station. It does **not** say the door was locked. Muirhead's 8 January 1901 report adds the rocket, east landing, stopped clock, repeat search, and temporary keepers. These support `[jjr5]`–`[njtt]`. [Northern Lighthouse Board, Moore letter and Muirhead report](https://www.nlb.org.uk/history/flannan-isles/).
- **Coat and departure order:** Moore says McArthur's usual wearing coat was left behind and infers that McArthur went out in shirt sleeves. Moore says Marshall's sea boots and oilskins, and Ducat's sea boots and old waterproof coat, were away. Muirhead relies on Moore's knowledge of when the men wore this gear to infer a trip near a landing. Neither document records who departed first or that McArthur followed the others to rescue them. Thus `[z4u3]`–`[3w38]` preserve the evidence/inference boundary; a coat cannot establish departure order. [Northern Lighthouse Board, Moore letter and Muirhead report](https://www.nlb.org.uk/history/flannan-isles/).
- **Date and west landing:** Muirhead gives a last log entry on 13 December, subsequent slate observations including 15 December, cleaned cookware, and a tidy kitchen; afternoon 15 December is his inference. His report distinguishes the later Archtor notice from the shore lookout's weather-limited observations. It records a 70-foot crane platform, a box about 110 feet above sea level, coiled ropes, rail damage, a displaced stone exceeding 20 cwt (about one metric tonne), and a lifebuoy torn from still-fastened ropes with canvas adhering. These are site/equipment heights, not a measured wave height. The large-wave account and the wind comparison are explicitly Muirhead's judgment, not eyewitness findings. These support `[cuxc]`–`[hpye]`. [Northern Lighthouse Board, Muirhead report](https://www.nlb.org.uk/history/flannan-isles/).
- **Poem and later exhibition:** The 1912 first-book text of Wilfrid Wilson Gibson's *Fires* contains “Flannan Isle” with an untouched meal and an overturned chair; Moore's letter does not record either, and Muirhead describes the kitchen as tidied. The National Records of Scotland announced on 3 August 2026 that Moore's letter was being displayed for the first time. These support `[i9ud]`–`[d4uq]`, provided the poem's image stays distinct from the 1900 evidence. [Gibson, *Fires Book I* (1912), digitized text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/42677/pg42677-images.html); [Northern Lighthouse Board primary-document transcripts](https://www.nlb.org.uk/history/flannan-isles/); [National Records of Scotland exhibition announcement](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/latest-news/scotland-s-lighthouse-history-in-exhibition/).

The NLB page publishes transcripts of the contemporary documents; this review did not inspect the manuscripts in person. NRS identifies the Muirhead report in its [NLC3/1/1 collection guide](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/lighthouses/?section=the-mystery-of-the-flannan-islands-lighthouse).
