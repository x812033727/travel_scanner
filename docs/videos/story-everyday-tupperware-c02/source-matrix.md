# C02 Tupperware — primary-source fact check (20 Oct 2026)

This matrix supports the story **「盒子還在，原公司為何走進重整？」**. “Tupperware” can mean the product brand, the public debtor Tupperware Brands Corporation, or a post-sale buyer; keep those meanings separate in narration. All cited pages below returned HTTP 200 from SEC EDGAR during this review.

| The product story begins in 1946; the public-company legal entity is later. | [2022 Form 10-K, Item 1, Description of Business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865423000079/tup-20221231.htm) (filed 13 Oct 2023) | “Founded in 1946, the Company’s signature container created the modern food storage category”; same section says Tupperware Brands Corporation “was organized on February 8, 1996.” | Say **brand/product founded 1946**, not that the bankrupt Delaware legal entity existed continuously since 1946. The 1946 wording is company self-description, not independently checked invention history. |
| Direct selling was central; digital and retail were being added. | [2022 Form 10-K, Item 1, Description of Business and Markets](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865423000079/tup-20221231.htm) | “The Company primarily uses a direct selling business model … while continuing to expand digital and retail distribution channels.” “Sales are to the ultimate customer principally through direct selling by an annual average active sales force of approximately 313 thousand in 2022.” Independent sellers bought at a discount, then sold to customers; many sold to friends, neighbors and relatives. | **313,000** is the annual *average active* independent sales force in **2022**, not a current headcount and not employees. “Home party” is supported by the company’s [17 Sep 2024 press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865424000068/exhibit991tupperwarepres.htm) (“made the home party famous”); avoid claiming party sales were the only channel. |
| The old model struggled before bankruptcy; retail growth could not offset the direct-channel decline. | [2022 Form 10-K, Item 1, Business Strategy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865423000079/tup-20221231.htm) | “While retail sales now represent four percent of the Company’s total revenue, positive results in this channel were not enough to offset declining trends in the direct selling channel.” Description of Business says the company had faced financial/operational headwinds since **2019**, and sales-force model/compensation changes did not deliver expected results. | **4% is 2022 share**; do not imply online retail caused the failure or that retail was absent. Filing also cites inflation, resin costs, China lockdowns, interest rates, FX and execution challenges. |
| Revenue declined in 2022 and through Q3 2023; going-concern concerns were explicit. | [2022 Form 10-K, Item 7, Net Sales and “Substantial Doubt Regarding … Going Concern”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865423000079/tup-20221231.htm); [Q3 2023 Form 10-Q, Item 2, Net Sales and Note 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865424000035/tup-20230930.htm) (filed 29 Mar 2024) | Annual sales: **US$1,304.0m in 2022 vs US$1,600.6m in 2021**. First 39 weeks: **US$828.3m in 2023 vs US$990.4m in comparable 2022 period**. The 10-K says uncertainty around liquidity, turnaround and loan covenants led to “substantial doubt” about ability to continue as going concern for at least one year from its issuance. Q3 filing repeats that conclusion. | First 39-week figures are **not full-year 2023 or 2024 sales**. The going-concern warning is an accounting conclusion before Chapter 11, not proof of liquidation then. Avoid reducing distress to one cause. |
| Debt pressure intensified in 2024. | [14 Aug 2024 Form 8-K, Item 1.01, Bridge Credit Agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865424000063/tup-20240812.htm) | On **12 Aug 2024**, company entered bridge facility of **up to US$8m**, **US$4m drawn** then, with **25% original-issue discount**, **14% annual interest**, and **30 Sep 2024 maturity**. Existing lenders extended forbearance to that maturity. | Good evidence of short-term financing strain; avoid asserting the loan alone caused bankruptcy or calling the full US$8m funded. Simpler narration: “八月還靠短期過橋貸款爭取時間.” |
| The listed parent and some subsidiaries filed Chapter 11; they proposed continued operation and sale. | [18 Sep 2024 Form 8-K, Item 1.03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865424000068/tup-20240917.htm); [Exhibit 99.1 press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000100865424000068/exhibit991tupperwarepres.htm) | Voluntary petitions filed **17 Sep 2024** in US Bankruptcy Court for District of Delaware; debtors sought to operate as debtors in possession during a sale process. Release says it intended to keep serving customers through consultants, retail partners and Tupperware.com while seeking court approval. | Say **entered Chapter 11**, not “products disappeared” or “all Tupperware companies worldwide went bankrupt.” Press-release continuation was an intention as of Sep 2024, not proof of current 2026 operations. |
| A lender-controlled buyer acquired specified business assets; old public-company stock and brand are distinct. | [29 Nov 2024 Form 8-K, Item 1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000119312524268115/d869890d8k.htm); [Asset Purchase Agreement, Section 1.1(n)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1008654/000119312524268115/d869890dex101.htm) | **22 Nov 2024** APA named **Party Products Holdings LLC** (parent) and **Party Products LLC** (purchaser), formed and controlled by the debtor’s lenders. Court approved on **24 Nov**; parties completed sale on **27 Nov**. Consideration was **US$53.8m credit bid plus cash equal to US$15.5m + Bridge Payoff Amount − Estimated Closing Cash**, plus assumed liabilities. APA includes sellers’ acquired intellectual property, including trademarks, in transferred asset categories. | Do not flatten consideration to “sold for US$53.8m” or “US$15.5m”: cash is formula-dependent and liabilities are assumed. Say **business/assets were sold**, not that the old listed corporation itself was bought or emerged from bankruptcy. The 8-K proves the 2024 closing; it does not establish the buyer’s current trade name, 2026 outlet footprint, or final Chapter 11 plan. |

## Current-status verification limit

SEC submissions for CIK 0001008654 end at the **29 Nov 2024** 8-K in the accessible index. The official Tupperware website (`https://www.tupperware.com/`) and court document agent (`https://dm.epiq11.com/Tupperware`) returned **CONNECT tunnel 403** from this cloud environment. The strongest verified post-sale statement is therefore: **the November 2024 asset sale to lender-controlled Party Products LLC closed**. A claim about exactly who operates every current 2026 Tupperware storefront, the debtor’s final legal disposition, or present-day sales should wait for another accessible primary source. The SEC APA’s schedules are omitted, so it does not prove every specific national trademark or contract transferred.
