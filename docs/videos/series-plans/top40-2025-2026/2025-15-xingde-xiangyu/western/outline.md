# Till the Verdict Do Us Part｜一季總綱

One evidence wall, one case tracker, one furnace: Shatter → Remelt → Bubbles → Quench → Clear. The evidence wall gains a few engraved glass panels each season (2→5→8→11→12); the beads in the tracker move one stage each season (Charges → Investigation → the Pell case reopened by the Conviction Integrity Unit → Arraignment → Sentencing); her name appears on something bigger each season (bubbles in a vase's base → first prize at Harbor Lights → the Remelt sign → the victim named in the charges → an entire corridor). He opens the doors; she walks through them herself. The marriage lasts till the day he is sentenced.

**結局**：As planned across five seasons (only season one, episodes 1–60, is in production; later seasons follow ../outline.json if season one performs): season five closes the main line. Grant Whitlock is sentenced to eleven years in state prison; Walter Pell's conviction is vacated after the prosecutor's Conviction Integrity Unit reviews the case; Kiln Row survives. The marriage contract expires on sentencing day: Elias keeps his word and leaves signed divorce papers on the table; Nora buys back her mother's land with her own money and slides a glass ring across to him — she proposes — and he brings out the crooked glass bird she blew at nineteen. The seventh-floor hallway at Merrow General is rebuilt in glass she designed, light running from one end to the other. No vigilantes, no accidents — the villain is brought down by a verdict read aloud. The last frame of episode 300: the torn last page of the furnace log arrives in her mail — a delivery receipt for the twelve lamps marked “Reproduction,” signed by Douglas Penrose, the arts commissioner cutting her ribbon on stage. “The Pell case isn't over.” A door left open for part two.

## 第 1 季《Shatter》（第 1–60 集）

主題：She loses her baby, her name and her home — then signs a marriage contract that ends the day he is sentenced.

開季：Port Merrow, the Glass House on the bluff that Grant Whitlock built for her. Nora Quinn is thirty-one, five months pregnant and high-risk; Dr. Brandt has prescribed prenatal medication. At nineteen she learned to blow glass from Walter Pell at Pell Glassworks on Kiln Row; in six years of marriage she hasn't touched a furnace. The Forever vase she blew at twenty-five as a wedding gift to her husband has become Whitlock Home's best seller, and her name isn't on the label. Her mother-in-law, Peg Whitlock, comes once a week to complain the soup is bland and the nursery window is too big. In episode 1 she dreams the Glass House shatters from the roof down, pane by pane, without a sound; a crooked glass bird falls and a hand catches it. She wakes, takes her pill, and says: “My pill tastes sweet today.”

季末：The sweet pill is explained (m01: not the medication Dr. Brandt prescribed but a lookalike sugar tablet; the hospital pharmacy pickup log shows the real pills were collected every month, signed for by Grant Whitlock). The signature on the property agreement is explained (m02: traced from the signature on her pre-marriage design sketches — every stroke hesitates and the flick at the end is missing; it is dated the night she was under anesthesia at Merrow General, and the transfer of the Forever design rights was traced by the same hand; the court-appointed examiner confirms the forgery, the court freezes the transfers of the Glass House and the accounts, and the forgery charge is filed). Why Peg left with her is explained (m03: the day Nora came home from the hospital, Peg found a confectionery receipt in her son's car — “Prop tablets, qty 100” — and overheard him on the phone, “That's taken care of”; thirty-four years ago she sat alone all night in the same hospital's hallway). Nora lost the baby in the seventh-floor hallway at Merrow General (only the hallway, a half-knitted baby hat and the flickering light at the end). The day she came home the locks were changed; attorney Leonard Fisk handed her the property agreement she had “signed,” giving Grant the house, the accounts and the Forever design rights, while Sienna Vale had workers hanging curtains in the Glass House. She took only her mother's glass block, the Keepsake, and Peg packed a suitcase and left with her. Deputy Prosecutor Marcus Reyes declined to file on her first report (“Half a pill isn't enough”). Elias Ashford handed her the three clauses outside the Harborview Motor Inn; she signed them and filed for divorce. Ninety days later, at Merrow County Superior Court, the judge entered the divorce decree — “I'll sign the divorce. The house — I'll see you in court.” — and across the hall at the County Clerk she and Elias took out a marriage licence, Peg and Bev from the motel as witnesses. Grant folded Sienna's marriage application into his pocket: “Some other day.” At the launch of the new Forever collection she used the spotlight to reveal a line of bubbles in the vase's base — her initials and their wedding date; in six years he had never once turned it over. Elias leased her the abandoned Pell Glassworks on Kiln Row; she recognized the place she had apprenticed at nineteen, and he said, “Bought it at a sheriff's sale.” Furnace No. 3 was lit for the first time in twelve years and the studio was named Remelt; the blind competition for the ferry terminal chandelier brought in the first commission; a glass-tube case tracker went up on the studio wall, its bead pushed from Report to Charges, and the first two panels went up on the evidence wall. In the last episode Grant walks into Remelt Studio for the first time, glances at the evidence wall and smiles: “Two panels of glass. Drop it, Nor. You can't beat me alone.” By the furnace, Elias turns, pulls off his heat glove: an old burn scar across the back of his left hand. Grant's smile freezes: “…You.” Elias says: “Hello, Sales. Twelve years.” Nora's blowpipe stops mid-air — only people from Pell's ever called him that.

### 篇章 1〈Sweet〉第 1–10 集｜Grief, played full、Mother-in-law alliance、Evidence revenge

Episode 1 cold open: a dream — the Glass House shatters from the roof down, pane by pane, without a sound; a crooked glass bird falls and a hand reaches in and catches it. She wakes; Grant has already left. On the table, a glass of warm water and one pill. She usually swallows it whole; today she bites it, frowns, and spits the other half into a tissue: “My pill tastes sweet today.” She calls him; he laughs: “They changed the coating, babe. Take it and rest.” Peg drops by to nitpick: “Soup's bland. And that nursery window — the whole street can see in.” That night she feels wrong, and the phone on her nightstand is gone — that morning he said he'd “get it fixed.” She holds onto the wall all the way out to the lamppost by the gate; a car stops, and a hand with a scar across the back of the left hand dials 911 (face never shown). Merrow General, the seventh-floor hallway, the light at the far end flickering. Dr. Brandt crouches down: “I'm so sorry.” Nora is still clutching the half-knitted baby hat; Nurse Kim gently tucks it into a bag. Grant arrives at dawn, umbrella still dripping: “We're still young.” The day she comes home, the locks on the Glass House have been changed. Leonard Fisk hands her two documents: divorce papers waiting for her signature, and a property agreement that already has it — “The house, the accounts, the Forever design rights. You already signed it all over to Mr. Whitlock, Mrs. Whitlock.” She says: “I never signed that.” Sienna has workers hanging curtains: “This house is way too see-through.” Nora takes one suitcase and her mother's glass block, the Keepsake. At the bottom of the drive Peg catches up, suitcase in hand: “I'm going with her.” Grant shouts from the door: “Mom!” Peg doesn't turn around: “Make your own dinner, Grant.”

- 短線：Why was the pill sweet?；Why did Peg leave with her?；Whose hand had the scar?
- 篇章結尾：The Harborview Motor Inn, two single beds. Peg writes in her ledger: “Room, eighty-nine.” Nora pulls a balled-up tissue from her coat pocket — inside is the half pill she spat out that morning. She touches it to her tongue. Sweet. Peg stares at the half pill, then looks away: “We see the doctor tomorrow.” Nora asks: “Peg, what do you know?” Peg switches off the lamp: “Go to sleep.”
- 奇觀：The Glass House shattering without a sound and the hand that catches the glass bird; the flickering light at the end of the hallway and the half-knitted hat; two women and two suitcases on the hill.

### 篇章 2〈Three Clauses〉第 11–20 集｜Evidence revenge、Standing beside her、Married first, in love later

Dr. Brandt lays the half pill under the lamp next to a real one: “What I prescribed doesn't taste sweet. And that's not the imprint.” Nora requests her own pickup records from the hospital pharmacy, and pharmacist Dale Hutchins prints five months of them: the real pills were collected every month, and every pickup is signed by the person who collected it — Grant Whitlock. The first big reversal: that night wasn't fate. She reports it to the police; Deputy Prosecutor Marcus Reyes reviews it and declines to file: “Half a pill and a pickup log don't prove he switched them. Bring me evidence, not a story.” Her accounts are frozen and her cards cancelled — and the bank dates show it was set up before that night. Peg pawns her own wedding ring, the one she has worn for forty years: “My ring. I'll pawn it if I want.” A black car has been parked across from the motel for five days. Bev, behind the front desk with her bowl of peppermints, points it out. The man inside is Elias Ashford, CEO of Ashford Construction; Nora has never met him. He hands her one page with three clauses: “One. This marriage ends on the day Grant Whitlock is sentenced. Two. I open doors — venues, lawyers, invitations. I don't design for you, testify for you, or decide for you. Three. When the Pell case goes to trial, you tell the truth.” She freezes — Pell's is where she apprenticed at nineteen: “What Pell case?” He says: “You'll find out.” She asks: “What do you want?” He says: “I want him in prison. He owes me.” Attorney Dana Okafor, leaning on the car, adds: “Two is mine. If he fights for you once, the defense calls you a bought witness.” Nora's last question: “Why me?” Elias: “Because you'll walk on your own.” Episode 19: she cuts her hair to her shoulders, signs the three clauses and files for divorce; Sienna posts a photo of a blank marriage application and a pen — “Soon.” Episode 20 opens: “Ninety days later.” A short montage — the motel room, Peg's ledger, physical therapy, Nora sketching at the motel table.

- 短線：What does Elias have against Grant?；What is the Pell case?；Will Sienna ever get her wedding?
- 篇章結尾：Merrow County Superior Court. Grant arrives with Leonard Fisk, expecting her to sign away the property along with the marriage. In the courtroom the judge enters the divorce decree; Nora's pen pauses over the page: “I'll sign the divorce. The house — I'll see you in court.” Then she stands and walks across the hall to the County Clerk's office, where Elias is already waiting for the marriage licence. Peg is the witness both times, Bev the second: “My daughter-in-law's divorce and her wedding. I'm signing for both.” Grant stares at her: “That fast?” Elias tucks her hand into the crook of his arm: “Mr. Whitlock. My wife says she doesn't know you.” Outside, Sienna is waiting with a marriage application and a pen; Grant walks over, folds the application twice and slides it into his inside pocket: “Some other day.”
- 奇觀：Two pills side by side under the lamp; the three clauses beside the black car; one courthouse hallway, two doors, the same witness.

### 篇章 3〈Forever〉第 21–30 集｜Public unmasking、Craft turns the tables、Standing beside her

She moves into the Ashford house — and brings her ex-mother-in-law. The house sits on the hill, grey board-formed concrete with very few windows; all Elias says is, “There's room.” Peg goes through the fridge: “You rich people just live on takeout?” The next night there is a pot roast and four sides on the table. Whitlock Home is launching a new Forever collection, and the invitation arrives at the Ashford house addressed to “Mrs. Ashford,” with a front-row seat. Nora says: “He wants to see what I wear to cry in.” Elias gives her the invitation and a mist-grey gown, and says: “You're the one on stage. I'll be in the audience.” Leonard Fisk sends a cease-and-desist letter. At the launch Grant calls Forever his tribute to “every woman who keeps a home,” and Sienna talks through the design inspiration. Nora walks up from the audience, asks Ray the lighting tech to bring the spotlight down low, picks up the sample vase — Forever No. 1 — and turns it upside down: “There's a name inside this vase.” In the base, a line of tiny bubbles catches the light and spells two initials and a date — the wedding gift she blew for him at twenty-five. Walter Pell taught her that: “Whatever you make, leave your name inside it.” Every phone in the room goes up. She looks at Grant: “Six years, and you never once turned it over.”

- 短線：Is the design-rights transfer real or fake?；How much does Sienna know?；How will Grant strike back?
- 篇章結尾：In the backstage hallway Grant blocks her way, smiling warmly: “You signed the rights to the company before the wedding. Your signature's on the transfer.” He hands her a copy. She looks at the signature for a long time: “That's not my signature.” Grant: “Then prove it.” At the end of the hallway Elias stands waiting for her and doesn't walk over — clause two.
- 奇觀：Bubbles in a vase's base spelling a name under the spotlight; a roomful of raised phones; the man at the end of the hallway who doesn't walk over.

### 篇章 4〈Remelt〉第 31–40 集｜Craft turns the tables、Grief, played full、Comedy

Dana files the forgery complaint, and Elias drives Nora to Kiln Row: a whole row of abandoned glassworks, faded letters on a steel door — “Pell Glassworks.” She gets out and stops dead: the summer she was nineteen, she learned glassblowing here from Walter Pell, and met the sales manager, Grant Whitlock; next door is her mother's shuttered studio, Claire Quinn Glass. She asks: “How do you own this place?” He says: “Bought it at a sheriff's sale.” Old furnace man Gus Moretti comes out and looks at her a long time: “You're that girl… outside the courthouse, talking to the reporters?” She says: “I said everything Walter made has his name inside it. Nobody listened.” Gus says nothing, turns and pulls the tarp off Furnace No. 3. Dr. Brandt has banned her from a hot furnace for six months, so she draws and Gus's grandson Sparks blows — and ruins twenty pieces; Peg keeps the books in the corner: “Sand's thirty-two bucks a bag. Blow carefully.” Nora insists on signing a lease with Ashford Construction at market rent: “Clause two. I pay for my own doors.” Theo works out the number and Peg drops her pen. Nora builds a case tracker on the wall from a glass tube: Report → Charges → Investigation → Arraignment → Trial → Sentencing, one glass bead sitting at Report. Furnace No. 3 is lit for the first time in twelve years, firelight on every face, and the studio gets its name: Remelt.

- 短線：Why did Elias buy Pell's?；The ferry terminal blind competition；Grant cuts off her supplies
- 篇章結尾：The ferry terminal wants a chandelier — a blind competition, no names on the entries — and she enters. The night before the deadline, Hank Doyle, the sand supplier, says he's out of stock; Whitlock Home is his biggest customer. Gus drags out sacks from the storeroom that haven't been opened in twelve years: “I still remember the Pell recipe.” Nora rolls up her sleeves: “Then we mix our own.” At dawn the first sheet of glass comes out of the furnace, exactly the blue she wanted.
- 奇觀：Faded “Pell Glassworks” lettering on Kiln Row; Furnace No. 3 lit for the first time in twelve years; sand sacks unopened for twelve years in the storeroom.

### 篇章 5〈Traced〉第 41–50 集｜Evidence revenge、Craft turns the tables、Married first, in love later

She tapes copies of the property agreement and the rights transfer to the light box and studies them stroke by stroke. “My signature flicks out at the end, like finishing a gather off the pipe.” She spreads out every design sketch from before the marriage — every signature flicks; the one on the agreement pauses at every stroke. Traced. Fisk argues her own sketches can't serve as samples, so she has to find a signature someone else kept — and Dana finds it: Nora and Grant's own marriage certificate from six years ago. Dana asks the court for a document examiner, and the court appoints Arthur Wexler, who comes to the studio and watches her sign ten times. Waiting for the report, she cooks pasta in the Ashford kitchen late one night, makes an extra plate and leaves it outside the study door; the next morning the plate is back, washed, with a note under it: “Salty.” She laughs for the first time since moving in. Theo finally gets to write it in his log. Grant tells the press she was “Divorced at noon, married to a billionaire by five,” and reporter Lyle Becker leads a pack to the corner of Kiln Row; the woman who runs the chowder shack on the corner runs them off with a ladle: “Go shoot your own family.” She looks at Nora twice and says nothing — she is Rosa Pell, Elias's mother. Then Fisk moves to replace the examiner for bias.

- 短線：The examiner's report；The note under the plate；Why did Rosa look at her twice?
- 篇章結尾：The motion to replace the examiner is denied, and the report comes back: the signatures on the property agreement and on the rights transfer were both traced, not written by her; the agreement is dated the night she was under anesthesia at Merrow General. The court freezes the transfers of the Glass House and the accounts, the prosecutor files the forgery charge, and the bead in the case tracker moves from Report to Charges. The report's last line: “The tracing was likely done left-handed.” She remembers Sienna at the launch, turning her notes with her left hand.
- 奇觀：Two signatures overlaid on the light box; the note under the plate that says “Salty”; the last line of the examiner's report.

### 篇章 6〈Twelve Years〉第 51–60 集｜Grief, played full、Mother-in-law alliance、Public unmasking

The first two panels go up on the evidence wall; she engraves them herself: “PILLS: pharmacy pickup log” and “SIGNATURE: examiner's report.” Peg stands in front of the wall a long time, then finally talks. The day Nora came home from the hospital, Peg found a receipt from a confectionery in her son's car — “Prop tablets, qty 100” — and that night she overheard him on the phone: “That's taken care of.” “Thirty-four years ago I sat all night in that same hallway at Merrow General. His father never came.” She puts the receipt on the table: “I'm not doing it for you. I'm doing it for the baby. I was one day late.” For the first time, Nora hugs her: “Mom.” Reyes looks at the receipt: “Who placed the order? No name. Still not enough.” The ferry's maiden-voyage night moves up with twenty-four blue panels still to blow, and Gus throws out his back with a furnace that can't go cold. Elias turns up after midnight in heat gloves and for seven nights does one thing only — tends the fire — while Nora calls the moves and Sparks blows panel after panel; the last one Sparks blows alone, and it is the first piece he has ever made that isn't crooked. On maiden-voyage night the twenty-four blue panels light up the terminal, the blind-competition envelope is opened in front of every camera — it reads “Remelt” — and Grant, the night's sponsor, has to say “Congratulations.” Afterwards he calls: “Drop the case and the house is yours.” Nora: “I don't need you to give it back. The court will.” Then a text: “Then I'll come by. Face to face.”
