# Till the Verdict Do Us Part｜設定集

Five months pregnant, Nora Quinn bit into her prenatal pill and tasted sugar. Her husband had switched it; she lost the baby in a hospital hallway. The day she came home the locks were changed, and a property agreement she never signed gave him everything. She left with one glass block of her mother's — and her mother-in-law, suitcase in hand, walked out with her. Then a CEO she had never met handed her a marriage contract with three clauses: “This marriage ends the day Grant Whitlock is sentenced. I open doors. You walk through them.”

## 前提

Nora Quinn is thirty-one. At nineteen she learned to blow glass from master Walter Pell at Pell Glassworks on Kiln Row; in six years of marriage she has not touched a furnace. Her husband Grant Whitlock turned her wedding gift to him, the Forever vase, into his home brand's best seller — without her name on it. He switched her prenatal pills and traced her signature, so that she lost the baby in a hospital hallway and walked out with nothing. Elias Ashford, CEO of Ashford Construction, offers her a revenge marriage: he gives her venues, lawyers and invitations, and never fights for her; she uses her own glass and her own evidence to send her ex-husband to prison by the book and burn her name back into her work. The contract expires the day Grant is sentenced in his criminal case. Elias keeps his own account: twelve years ago, in the Pell Glassworks antique-lamp case, his father took the fall for Grant. And he keeps his own secret: twelve years ago he was the soot-faced boy at the furnace, “Soot,” who caught a falling gather of hot glass that was meant for her. Five seasons: Shatter, Remelt, Bubbles, Quench, Clear.

## 世界

- **The Glass House**：The house Grant built for her on the bluff over the water: glass on every side, anyone can see in. In season one Sienna hangs heavy curtains; in season two the court gives it back to Nora; the season-five reconciliation dinner happens here.
- **Merrow General, seventh-floor hallway**：Where she lost the baby. The light at the far end never stops flickering. In season five it is torn out and replaced with a glass corridor she designed.
- **Kiln Row — Pell Glassworks (Remelt Studio)**：The glassworks where she apprenticed at nineteen, closed twelve years ago. Elias bought it back and leases it to her; Furnace No. 3 is lit again. On the wall: the evidence wall and the case tracker, a glass tube with beads. Next door is her mother's shuttered studio, Claire Quinn Glass.
- **The Ashford house on the hill**：Grey board-formed concrete, very few windows. She moves into the guest room with her ex-mother-in-law; the long dining table is the two mothers' battlefield. In season five it gains a window she blew.
- **Whitlock Home flagship store**：Grant's brand. The Forever vase sits in the brightest case; the launch stage and the shareholder meetings are here.
- **Merrow County Prosecutor's Office and Superior Court**：Deputy Prosecutor Reyes's office, the civil courtroom and the main courtroom; the case moves one stage forward each season.
- **Port Merrow Museum of Art, storage**：Where twelve “antique” lamps were bought twelve years ago; the last one is still locked in storage.
- **Rosa's**：A chowder shack at the corner of Kiln Row that Rosa Pell has kept open for twelve years; where the two mothers make peace.

## 調性

- **Evidence revenge**：She wins only by evidence and procedure: the pharmacy pickup log, the examiner's report, bubbles, polarized light, the furnace log, a voice memo. At least one piece of evidence lands per chapter, and a few engraved panels go up on the evidence wall each season. No vigilante justice, nothing obtained illegally.
- **Public unmasking**：Launches, press conferences, shareholder meetings, dinners: the lie collapses in front of a crowd — the evidence comes out, the other side lets something slip. Only fakes get smashed, never people.
- **Craft turns the tables**：She wins with glass: the bubble signature, live glassblowing, polarized light, fusing and opening seals. She only enters blind competitions and never uses the name Mrs. Ashford; he never designs or decides for her.
- **Standing beside her**：In public he steps up next to her — “It's a blind competition. I don't even know which piece is hers.” — and then steps back. He opens doors; she walks through them.
- **Married first, in love later**：Three clauses, separate rooms, one long dining table. The feeling moves one notch at a time: a plate of pasta, a note under it that says “Salty,” a light left on, a name that slips out.
- **Secret devotion**：Twelve years of quiet love hidden in objects: the burn scar, “Soot,” the crooked glass bird, the man who sat all night at the end of the hallway, an unsent letter. A little shows each season; season five lays it all out.
- **Mother-in-law alliance**：Her ex-mother-in-law packs a suitcase and leaves with her — sharp-tongued, good with numbers, fiercely loyal. From season two the two mothers, Peg and Rosa, go from bickering to teaming up; the warmth and the laughs live at the dinner table.
