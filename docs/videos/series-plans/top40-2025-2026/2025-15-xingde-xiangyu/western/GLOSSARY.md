`../outline.json`, `../season-01.json`) is the story; this file says what each Chinese name, place,
object and procedure becomes. Where the Chinese text and this file disagree, this file wins. Where this
file is silent, adapt the Chinese faithfully and naturally for an American audience — do not invent
new plot.

## Tone of the English

- Dialogue is spoken American English: short, concrete, a line under about ten words. No translated
  idioms ("my mother-in-law's tofu-knife mouth"); find the English equivalent or cut it.
- Owner 2026-11-10: **no need for restraint, tears or comfort; only avoid YouTube's yellow icon.** Grief,
  rage and humiliation play at full volume (sobbing, screaming, a slap, a thrown drink, handcuffs). Still never
  graphic: no blood, no medical procedure, no ultrasound, no self-harm, no sexual content, no f-words. Never write
  "miscarriage", "abortion", "blood", "suicide", "kill", "dead baby", "sexy", "nude" in a `youtube_title`.
  No line says "dead baby" or the baby's name; "my baby", "our baby", "my grandbaby" can be shouted.
- Money is US dollars. Dates and seasons follow the Pacific Northwest (rain, ferries, grey water).
- **Numbers and times in dialogue are written as words** (2026-11-12): `nine-one-one`, `Four-one-one-six`, `March twelfth`,
  `ten o'clock`, `ten-fifteen`, `Night ninety`, `Eleven-eight`, `Entry nine`, `Day forty-two`. Digits get drawn as text.
- **Forever No. 1 is spoken "Forever Number One" / "Number One"** (2026-11-12); "No. 1" only in Chinese notes.
- **Never say or write "tablet" for the pill** (2026-11-12): the model draws a tablet computer. It is "pill",
  "my prenatal medication", "the pills Dr. Brandt prescribed". The candy-shop receipt is read aloud as "Prop pills."
- Nothing readable is generated or added in post (2026-11-12, all bibles): signs, phone screens, texts, signatures,
  the ledger, the case-tracker stages, engraved panels, receipts. If the audience needs it, a character says it.
  The one exception is the "N.Q." of light dots in a single locked still in episode 28.

## Procedure: write it this way (not the Chinese way)

1. **No same-day divorce and remarriage (episodes 19–20)** — **(2026-11-12, owner chose option A; follow `s1-bible-e011-e020.md`)**.
   Washington has a 90-day waiting period. Episode 19 ends with Nora signing the three clauses at dawn and filing for divorce at the
   Superior Court clerk's window on day 9: "Filed." Dana: "Ninety days. Earliest decree." Episode 20 is **day 99**. There is **no
   "Ninety days later" card**: the time passes in a four-shot opening montage (April rain, Nora sells a harbor drawing at a gift shop;
   May sun, she runs up the seawall stairs; June dawn, Peg beside the finished grey-blue scarf reads her ledger: "Night ninety." "Nora,
   paid."; two packed suitcases by the door). The earlier physical-therapy shot and the marriage application are cut. Then the courthouse:
   the judge enters the divorce decree in one courtroom (Dana beside Nora; Peg and Bev in the gallery: a decree needs no witness).
   "I'll sign the divorce." "The house — I'll see you in court." Across the hall, a **different judge** marries them in a small courtroom;
   **Peg and Bev sign the two witness lines** (Peg: "Divorce or wedding, I'm in her corner. Both."). Nobody mentions a licence or the
   three-day wait, and no shot shows one: this is the series' one deliberate dramatic licence. (The old text — the licence wait passing
   during the ninety days, Peg as the only witness — is superseded.)
2. **Dr. Brandt's ban** is "no hot furnace for **six months**", counted from the night she lost the baby (day 2) to about day 186, so it
   is still in force when Kiln Row opens (chapter 4) and lifts early in what would be season two. **(2026-11-12)** It is spoken only in
   ep 33: "No heat, no fumes, no heavy lifting. Six months." Nora: "So ten more weeks." Until then nobody mentions it. On screen Nora
   never crosses the yellow line (once, the ep 33 cliffhanger), never holds a hot pipe or opens the furnace, never lifts sacks or crates;
   in eps 59–60 her blowpipe is cold and empty.
3. **Reporting the crime.** **(2026-11-12)** Nora reports to the Port Merrow Police; the detective phones Deputy Prosecutor Marcus Reyes
   from his desk on speaker (ep 14), and Reyes **declines to file**: "So. Half a pill and a pickup log." "That doesn't prove he switched
   them." Nora: "He took my baby! What else do you need?" "Evidence. Not a story." "What would be enough?" "Something that puts his
   hand on that sugar pill." (The formal declination letter comes later and is not shown.) The forgery is reported by Dana on day 109
   ("Prosecutor's office called. Reyes will review it.", ep 34); in ep 50 the forgery charge is **filed** against Grant Whitlock and the
   case tracker bead moves from *Report* to *Charges* ("Charges."). In ep 53 Reyes takes Peg's receipt in person: "Order number's real."
   "I'll subpoena the shop."
4. **Pharmacy records.** Nora requests **her own** pickup records from the hospital pharmacy herself ("The pharmacy logs who signs for
   every pickup." "You can ask for your own."); each pickup carries the signature of the person who collected it: "Every month. Same
   signature. Grant Whitlock." The pill itself goes to an outside lab under a chain-of-custody form ("Sign here. So nobody can say we
   swapped it." "Outside lab. Three days."); the result: "No active ingredient. It's sugar." Grant gets nothing: "HIPAA. He gets nothing."
5. **Property.** Washington is a community-property state: the forged "separate property agreement"
   gives Grant the Glass House, the accounts and the Forever design rights. In the divorce Nora is the **petitioner** and Grant the
