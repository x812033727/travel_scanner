## Six-chapter path, callbacks, and answers

1. **The empty rack:** A specific black-pants rack vanishes in March. The opening holds back three answers: what April found in the old inspection, how long the rack stayed empty, and where the affected goods entered the first-quarter accounts.
2. **Swatch to garment:** Third-party fabric checks, outside production, and an older standard show why a tested swatch is not the same object as a worn pair of pants. The batch moves toward the store, leaving the exact first-sale date open.
3. **March withdrawal:** March 1 answers that date. The company withdraws affected items from stores, showrooms, and online sale, then distinguishes its early lost-sales forecasts from goods already made and held in stock. The investigation remains open.
4. **April findings:** The company says the fabric might have met the old range while some fabric variables were not fully tested; small pattern changes also mattered. New material checks and factory oversight follow. The chapter turns from fixing the process to the time needed to get pants back on the rack.
5. **Return and quarter-end accounts:** The first quarter's last seven weeks lacked the product; the later announcement says Luon returned within 90 days of withdrawal. A US$17.5m inventory reserve entered cost of goods sold while company revenue increased. The gross-margin discussion limits what can be assigned to the pants, and the chapter asks whether the full-year true lost sales can be calculated.
6. **Annual-report limit:** The next report aggregates products and periods rather than disclosing a final sales-loss line for this batch. Its absent figure answers the last question, and the ending explicitly returns to the opening rack and the swatch-versus-garment distinction.
