## Thirteen blind comprehension answers

These are answers a first-time listener can derive from the script alone; any missing answer is marked as such.

1. **Where is the opening action?** Outside Cleopatra's guarded tomb in Alexandria; a villager brings figs to the guards (L9–13).
2. **Does the opening establish a snake as observed fact?** No. It says “有一種說法” and asks whether the guards missed one (L17).
3. **Why does Octavian want her alive?** Plutarch's stated motives are preserving tomb treasure and capturing her; the script also describes a triumph with her as captive (L43–47).
4. **How does Proculeius enter?** He uses a ladder and the high window where Antony had been drawn in (L63–71).
5. **What other death attempt is reported?** Plutarch attributes a fasting attempt to the physician Olympus's account; Octavian suspects it and invokes her children's safety to make her eat (L89–97).
6. **Did her children keep the throne she requested?** The script does not say. It gives the request at L55 and later says she and the children are to be moved within three days at L143.
7. **Who warns her of the planned departure?** Dolabella, a young man close to Octavian, sends news at her request (L133–143).
8. **What does the guard actually see in the basket?** He sees figs after leaves are moved, praises them, takes none, and admits the basket (L175–187).
9. **What does the sealed letter ask?** Burial beside Antony, which Octavian later permits (L193–205, L225).
10. **Who is present when the messengers enter?** Cleopatra is dead in royal dress, Iras is dying at the bed's foot, and Charmion is adjusting the head ornament and then collapses (L213–221).
11. **Does Plutarch decide the means of death?** No. The fig-basket snake, vessel snake, and poisoned hairpin are competing reports; he says what happened is unknown (L239–259).
12. **How does Dio's account differ?** He puts Cleopatra in the palace with her usual attendants, describes light arm marks, and relays snake and poisoned hairpin accounts without deciding (L289–313).
13. **Does the triumph statue prove a bite?** No. It shows a snake image had entered Octavian's public victory display; the script explicitly separates that image from seeing a bite, and says none of these three authors gives certainty (L335–347).
