2. **What is the basket's disputed significance?** One later account says an asp was concealed among figs and leaves; the script does not present a guard as seeing it [cl003, cl054–cl056].
3. **How does Antony reach Cleopatra, and where does he die?** Wounded Antony is brought below the tomb, Cleopatra and two maids haul him up with ropes, and Plutarch says he dies beside her [cl006–cl008].
4. **What does Octavian seek?** He wants the tomb's treasure and Cleopatra alive as a potential captive in his triumph [cl009–cl010].
5. **How does Proculeius reach and restrain her?** After talk at the door, he and two others use a ladder at the high window; he takes her dagger and checks her clothing for poison [cl011–cl017].
6. **How is her fasting attempt stopped?** Plutarch attributes the account to her physician Olympus; Octavian invokes danger to her children and she eats again [cl019–cl021].
7. **Why does the inventory dispute matter?** A steward says valuables are omitted, Cleopatra says they are gifts for Octavian's family to plead for her, and Octavian takes it as a desire to live [cl025–cl028].
8. **What does Dolabella secretly report?** Octavian plans to return via Syria and move Cleopatra and her children within three days [cl029–cl031].
9. **What does Cleopatra do before the final meal?** She visits Antony's grave with two maids, lays a wreath and kisses it, then bathes and eats [cl033–cl038].
10. **What does the sealed letter request?** It asks Octavian to bury her with Antony; he understands the implication and sends people back [cl043, cl046–cl047].
11. **What do the messengers find?** Cleopatra is dead in royal dress, Iras is dying by the bed, and Charmion is adjusting Cleopatra's diadem before she falls; no survivor explains the act [cl048–cl053].
12. **Which methods and signs does Plutarch report?** He lists an asp in figs, a snake in a vessel prodded with a golden spindle, and poison in a hollow hairpin, while saying the truth is unknown; he reports no conspicuous poison signs or found snake and only alleged tracks and faint punctures [cl054–cl064].
13. **What do Dio, Suetonius, and the triumph image add?** Dio says the method is unknown while reporting snake in a water jar or bouquet and poisoned hairpin rumors [cl070–cl075]; Suetonius says she was *believed* snake-bitten and Octavian sought the Psylli [cl077–cl078]; the triumph displayed a snake-bearing Cleopatra image, which establishes a public representation but not eyewitness proof [cl080–cl083].
