An open fridge door doesn't cool the room. So why not? | So That's Why｜CC Subtitles

On a sweltering afternoon, Little Q opens the fridge door and feels cool air on the face right away.
In this episode, the Chief and Little Q watch the same fridge and draw their circle bigger and bigger: first just the inside of the fridge, to ask whether cold is really something that leaks out; then round the back, to see how the refrigerant and the compressor carry heat out loop after loop, and where the compressor's electricity goes; then a circle around the whole kitchen to tally up the heat; and finally out to the balcony, to see why the air conditioner's outdoor unit blows hot air, and why an air conditioner can still cool a room.

So if the fridge door stays open all day, can the kitchen skip the air conditioner?

The illustrations in this episode were made with AI. The "two balls of heat plus one of electricity" in the video are easy numbers assumed for the sake of the math; how many times more heat an air conditioner moves than the power it uses is a rough textbook figure, and the real number depends on the model and the weather. The source of every claim is listed in the references below.
