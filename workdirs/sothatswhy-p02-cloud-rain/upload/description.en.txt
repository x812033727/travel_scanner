A cloud holds about 500 tonnes of water. Why doesn't it fall? | So That's Why｜CC Subtitles

At 2 p.m.
In this episode, the Chief and Little Q start from a single drop of water in a cloud: how water vapour differs from a cloud, why a cloud droplet falls only about 1 cm a second, why one raindrop takes about a million cloud droplets, why thunderstorms come in the afternoon, and why they often stop so soon. At the end, three things to watch for when you look up.

on a summer afternoon the sun is blazing; by 3 p.m. it's pouring. That cloud you see when you look up really does hold water: an ordinary cumulus cloud has an estimated 500 tonnes or so. Water is heavier than air, so how does it stay up in the sky? And how does it turn into the heavy rain hitting you in under an hour?

The illustrations in this episode were made with AI. The numbers are estimates for typical cases, and real clouds vary a lot; the source of every number is listed in the references below.
